# HATAB editorial visual system

## Direction

The storefront should feel like a real Egyptian office-furniture studio: calm, useful, tactile, and confident. The system uses warm paper and pale stone as the resting field, deep forest as the structural colour, oxblood for a small number of actions, and brass only for technical accents.

The intended visual ratio is approximately 70% light neutral, 20% forest/ink, and 10% accent. Photography should alternate between a complete workspace, a clean product or movement study, and a material/detail frame. Avoid consecutive dark scenes, permanent decorative motion, excessive pills, glass effects, or multiple competing overlays.

## Generated reception concept

The reception scene was generated as a missing category/collection concept, not as project evidence. Exact final prompt:

```text
Use case: photorealistic-natural
Asset type: wide website category and collection banner for an Egyptian premium office-furniture brand
Primary request: Create an authentic, editorial photograph of a contemporary office reception and waiting lounge in Cairo that feels designed and built by a real local furniture studio.
Scene/backdrop: calm sunlit reception interior with limestone-toned walls, pale terrazzo floor, a custom walnut reception desk, two refined upholstered waiting chairs, a low side table, one architectural plant, subtle workspace visible beyond a glass partition.
Subject: the furniture and spatial composition, with one naturally dressed Egyptian professional walking softly in the far background, slightly motion-blurred and not posing.
Style/medium: high-end architectural interior photography, physically plausible, real materials and small natural imperfections; not a CGI render.
Composition/framing: wide landscape banner around 2:1, eye-level 28mm architectural lens, calm asymmetric composition, reception desk and chairs concentrated on the right two-thirds, generous quiet negative space on the left for bilingual website copy, level verticals.
Lighting/mood: soft late-morning Cairo daylight, gentle warm shadows, airy and reassuring, restrained contrast.
Color palette: warm off-white, pale stone, walnut brown, deep forest green, with one small muted oxblood upholstery accent and tiny brushed-brass details.
Materials/textures: visible walnut grain, woven wool upholstery, honed stone, matte powder-coated metal, clear glass.
Constraints: no text, no visible logo, no watermark, no brand-name furniture, no exaggerated curves, no impossible geometry, no empty sterile showroom, no overly perfect surfaces.
Avoid: glossy 3D render look, fantasy architecture, excessive decor, hotel lobby styling, saturated teal-orange grading, duplicated objects, distorted chairs, dramatic cinematic fog.
```

The dedicated waiting/lounge scene used this exact final prompt:

```text
Use case: photorealistic-natural

```

## CMS and data precedence

- Catalog default hero: `catalog.hero.image`.
- Category hero: `catalog.category.{slug}.banner`, with `Category.image` retained as an entity-level fallback.
- Collections hero and motion: `collections.hero.image` and `collections.motion.video`.
- Style edits: `collections.style.{slug}.image`.
- Homepage space, category, coordination, process, and motion slots live under `media.home`.
- Portfolio reference and CTA: `portfolio.hero.image` and `portfolio.ctaBanner.image`.

Active CMS media overrides win over bundled defaults. Every editorial image slot stores bilingual alt text and optional JSON metadata such as `{"objectPosition":"62% 50%"}`. The content dashboard exposes this position as the visual focal-point field.

## Production gate

Editorial banners do not bypass catalog quality controls. Products still need verified content and approved assets before production publication. Collections should not be activated until their items and copy are complete. Generated editorial concepts may support navigation and art direction, but they cannot satisfy product-image or project-proof requirements.
